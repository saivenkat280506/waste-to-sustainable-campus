Unit I

Natural Resources

Use materials efficiently and reduce demand for virgin resources.

Every sheet of paper and every plastic container thrown away is a small request for raw material that the earth has to provide. Resource use is easiest to fix at the moment of consumption — before the item is even bought, printed or served.

  • Reduce. Decrease avoidable single-use waste and unnecessary paper use.
  • Reuse first. A paper sheet reused as a rough-work sheet, and a container reused as a pot, keeps demand down without asking anything of the supply chain.
  • Recycle what is left. Send suitable paper and plastic to appropriate recycling channels instead of discarding it.
Unit II

Ecosystems

Prevent litter and reduce the risk of waste reaching soil and water.

A campus is part of a much larger ecological system. What is dropped on the ground does not stay on the ground — rain carries litter down through topsoil and subsoil towards streams, where it affects soil quality, plant life and aquatic organisms.

  • Segregate at source. Keep paper, clean plastic and wet waste in separate labelled bins so nothing leaks into the wrong stream.
  • Contain it. Use covered containers, set collection times and keep collection and storage areas tidy.
  • Never process on the ground. No melting, burning or open processing of plastic on campus.
Unit III

Biodiversity

Cleaner surroundings help protect habitats and living organisms.

A campus with less litter is a campus where plants get a chance, insects are not working around plastic, birds are not feeding on packaging, and the green spaces between the buildings actually function as habitat rather than as a backdrop for rubbish.

  • Protect the green areas. Cleaner surroundings help protect habitats and living organisms around the campus.
  • Give waste a growing use. Seed paper and plant pots and trays turn recovered material into something that supports plant life.
  • Awareness is part of the ecology. Posters and student volunteers build the habit that keeps litter out of green spaces in the first place.
Unit IV

Environmental Pollution

Better segregation and recycling can reduce pollution and open dumping.

Pollution on a campus is rarely dramatic. It is a stack of plastic cups near the canteen, a heap of mixed bags at the gate, and an open dump at the edge of the campus. Segregation is the first and cheapest intervention against all three.

Less litter in the open

Labelled bins at canteens, classrooms and offices keep litter from accumulating in the first place.

Cleaner recycling streams

Keeping paper, clean plastic and wet waste apart reduces contamination, so what is sent for recycling is actually usable.

Less open dumping

Coordinate with authorised recyclers and set collection times, so material leaves the campus through a proper channel.

The safety line

Better segregation reduces pollution only when it is paired with safe handling — see the risk assessment and simplified EIA for the controls we set on every activity.

Unit V

Social Issues & Sustainability

Build participation, responsible consumption and community action — the human half of the environmental half.

What sustainable development means

Meeting present needs while protecting resources and opportunities for the future.

Who has to cooperate

A campus waste plan works best when students, faculty, housekeeping staff and waste collectors cooperate.

Project action — awareness posters

Posters at the bins so the labels explain themselves, and in common areas so the habit is visible.

Project action — student volunteers

A small group who keep the routine running and support collection times.

Project action — labelled bins

One stream per bin, clearly marked, at every waste point on campus.

Project action — periodic waste checks

Regular reviews of what the bins actually contain, so the plan can be corrected.

Expected benefit

Responsible consumption habits that can continue beyond a single project.
