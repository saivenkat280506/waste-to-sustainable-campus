Chapter four

Potential risks, practical controls, and what we monitor

A waste plan that ignores safety is a liability. Here is every risk we identified in our own activities, the control we have put against it, and the simplified EIA that keeps us honest.

Potential risks & practical controls

Five risks, five controls

Open a row to see what it leads to and what we do about it. Each control is something a student, a volunteer or the housekeeping team can follow without special equipment.

What it leads to

Contamination and odour. Mixed paper and plastic cannot be recycled properly, and damp or dirty material attracts flies and smells.

Control

Labelled bins; keep paper and plastic clean and dry.

What it leads to

Risk of cuts or contact with unsafe materials, especially when students sort without knowing what a bin actually contains.

Control

Do not handle manually; inform campus staff.

What it leads to

Fumes, heat or unsafe DIY methods. Melting plastic on campus releases harmful vapours and creates a fire and burn hazard.

Control

Never melt or burn plastic on campus; use approved recyclers or safe reuse methods.

What it leads to

Unsuitable seeds spread beyond the campus, or pulp handled in ways that are unhygienic or unsupervised.

Control

Use clean recycled pulp and locally suitable, non-invasive seeds with guidance.

What it leads to

Overflow, scattered waste and unhygienic areas — the point at which a tidy plan turns into a dirty campus.

Control

Set collection times, use covered containers and keep areas tidy.

The short version

If a volunteer remembers nothing else, these are the three lines that keep the project safe and effective.

Keep it clean and dry Never melt plastic Call staff for sharp waste
Three hard rules

Kept on the wall beside the bins

Three lines, printed at every bin station. They cover almost every risk on this page.

Always

Keep it clean and dry

Paper and plastic that are clean and dry can be recycled properly. Contamination is what makes a whole bin useless.

Never

Melt or burn plastic

Fumes and heat make unsafe DIY methods unsafe. Use approved recyclers, or reuse plastic by cutting it instead.

Always

Call staff for sharp waste

Sharp or unknown waste is not handled manually. Inform campus staff and let the people trained for it take over.

Simplified EIA / EMP

What we monitor while the plan runs

A simplified Environmental Impact Assessment for a project this size is simple and honest: watch three things, review them regularly, and change the plan when they drift.

Those three indicators tell us whether the plan is working, whether it is being used at all, and whether the material we recover is actually good enough to recycle.

01
Indicator

Bin use

Are the labelled bins actually being used, and are they being used correctly?

02
Indicator

Contamination

Is mixed or dirty waste still entering the paper and plastic streams?

03
Indicator

Quantities recycled

How much material actually left the campus through a proper recycling channel?

Environmental management plan
  • Periodic waste checks record what the bins actually look like.
  • Awareness posters are updated where misuse keeps happening.
  • Student volunteers support collection times and keep areas tidy.
  • Authorised recyclers confirm where recovered material actually goes.
  • Review closes the loop — the project link ends with a review, not a launch.

Reduce · Segregate · Reuse · Recycle · Review

The five steps are a cycle, not a checklist. Review sends the learning back to the start.
